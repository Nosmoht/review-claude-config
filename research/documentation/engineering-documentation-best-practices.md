## Core Principles

### Hyperlink Everything
"The more you hyperlink, the more likely that someone will find the document they are looking for" ([Koliber](https://koliber.com/articles/engineering-documentation-best-practices)). Link to source material, related docs, code files, external references, and decision records. This makes claims verifiable and lets readers go deeper.

### Document Decision Rationale
Engineers need a record of decisions and design choices made during development. Document not just WHAT was decided but WHY — the constraints, tradeoffs, evidence, and alternatives considered. This is valuable when team members need to understand why features were built in certain ways ([Stepsize](https://www.stepsize.com/blog/the-engineers-complete-guide-to-writing-excellent-internal-documentation)).

### Discoverability Over Creation
"Don't think about who will write the documentation and how they will write it. Think of who will look for information and how will they find it" ([Koliber](https://koliber.com/articles/engineering-documentation-best-practices)). Organize by topic (hub-based architecture), not chronologically. Support both navigation and search.

### Audience-First Writing
Prevent the "curse of knowledge" where experts write documentation unintelligible to newcomers. Think about who reads it and what they need. A user-first approach leads to faster adoption, reduced support burden, and increased satisfaction.

### Living Documents
Documentation should be updated as part of daily workflow, not treated as a one-time artifact. Assign ownership, conduct regular reviews, and enforce a culture where each new hire either makes one edit or identifies one improvement during onboarding ([Koliber](https://koliber.com/articles/engineering-documentation-best-practices)).

## Structural Best Practices

### Hub-Based Organization
Organize through interconnected hubs: team hubs, functional area hubs (DevOps, QA), system hubs, process hubs, project hubs. "Don't limit yourself to a hierarchy" — use hierarchies, hyperlinking, tagging, and search simultaneously ([Koliber](https://koliber.com/articles/engineering-documentation-best-practices)).

### Precision and Completeness
Include all procedural steps and specific details. Define formats, styles, and role responsibilities. "Every step should be documented in enough detail that someone unfamiliar could execute it" ([Stepsize](https://www.stepsize.com/blog/the-engineers-complete-guide-to-writing-excellent-internal-documentation)).

### Visual Communication
Use diagrams, flowcharts, tables, and screenshots. Visual explanations communicate processes more effectively than lengthy narratives and combat documentation fatigue.

## Common Mistakes

- Allowing documentation to become stale without maintenance
- Creating silos of unused, undiscoverable information
- Writing without sources — making claims unverifiable
- Attempting to consolidate everything into a single system or document
- Writing for yourself instead of for your audience
